# Home Energy Watch Marketing Materials

## The position to lead with

Home Energy Watch helps people turn utility interval data into a clear, reviewable record of how a home is using electricity.

The strongest first audience is a homeowner who feels that the bill no longer matches the household. The next audiences are advocates or reviewers handling several accounts, and commissions or agencies that need a consistent record for follow-up.

Use this language consistently:

> A flagged pattern is a reason to look more closely, not proof of theft or tampering.

Avoid promising that the product catches thieves, proves wrongdoing, identifies a person, or settles a dispute. The product measures the usage history in the file, explains the rule that produced a flag, and keeps the supporting record together.

1. **Business email on the domain**
   - Suggested addresses: `hello@homeenergywatch.com`, `support@homeenergywatch.com`, and `privacy@homeenergywatch.com`.
   - Use one business-owned mailbox for account ownership and recovery. Do not use a personal social login as the only owner.

2. **Meta Business Suite**
   - Create or claim the Home Energy Watch Facebook Page.
   - Create an Instagram professional account and connect it to the Page.
   - Create the Meta business portfolio and ad account, even if ads come later.
   - Turn on two-step verification and give people individual access instead of sharing a password.
   - Claim `@HomeEnergyWatch` on both platforms if available.

3. **Google owner account**
   - Create or confirm a Google Analytics 4 property for `homeenergywatch.com`.
   - Verify both the marketing domain and the app domain in Google Search Console.
   - Submit the sitemap and record the conversion pages: signup, upload, pricing, and contact.

4. **Email list**
   - Use a permission-based email service with a signup form, unsubscribe link, and a simple welcome sequence.
   - Start with one list: people who want the homeowner export checklist and product updates.

5. **YouTube channel**
   - Reserve the Home Energy Watch name.
   - Use it for short screen recordings: downloading an export, uploading it, reading the overnight baseline, and opening a flagged day.

### Add when the first audience is clear

- **LinkedIn Company Page:** useful for reviewer, advocate, commission, and agency conversations.
- **Google Ads account:** wait until Search Console shows the questions people use and the signup path is measurable.
- **Google Business Profile:** only if the business meets Google's in-person or service-area eligibility rules. An online-only business should not create one.
- **TikTok:** optional. Use it only if you are willing to make short educational videos regularly.

## How to market it

### Audience one: homeowners

Speak to the moment when the bill feels out of step with the house:

- “What changed while the house was asleep?”
- “See the overnight pattern instead of guessing from the total bill.”
- “Bring a dated, readable record to the next utility conversation.”

Invite the reader to start with one account and the latest utility export. Do not make the reader understand kilowatts before they begin.

### Audience two: reviewers and advocates

Speak to the work of keeping several cases organized:

- “Keep each account’s files, thresholds, notes, and reports together.”
- “Compare periods without losing the date alignment.”
- “Give the next conversation a record people can read.”

Send this audience to the Review Desk page and ask them to request a conversation when they need several accounts.

### Audience three: commissions and agencies

Speak to consistency and follow-up:

- “Start with the source file.”
- “Show which rule produced each flag.”
- “Keep the record clear enough to revisit later.”

Use a direct conversation request rather than pushing a self-serve subscription before the agency offer is defined.

## First 30 days

### Week one: make the path trustworthy

- Finish the business email, Meta, Google, email-list, and YouTube accounts.
- Confirm the public domain resolves outside the development environment.
- Test the path from Facebook post to marketing page to signup to upload.
- Add measurement for signup starts, completed signups, uploads, pricing views, and contact requests.
- Publish one plain-language export guide.

### Weeks two and three: teach before selling

- Publish three Facebook posts each week.
- Reuse the strongest posts on Instagram with a simple chart or screen recording.
- Publish one short video each week.
- Answer comments with useful explanations, not a hard sell.
- Ask early users which phrase brought them to the site and what stopped them from uploading.

### Week four: test a small paid campaign

Start with one homeowner campaign and one reviewer campaign. Send each to the page that matches the audience. Pause any ad that produces clicks without signup starts or useful questions.

Do not scale paid traffic until the public site, signup flow, pricing, privacy explanation, and upload experience have been checked from an ordinary browser.

## What success should look like

For the first month, I would judge this by useful behavior rather than follower count:

- People understand the offer without needing the word “theft” to carry the message.
- Qualified visitors reach the correct audience page.
- Homeowners create accounts and upload a utility export.
- A small number of people pay for Home Watch because the record is useful.
- Reviewers or advocates ask about the multi-account workspace.
- At least one commission or agency conversation begins with a real review need.
- Questions from visitors reveal exactly where the product or copy is unclear.

The first practical target is ten real users who complete the path from interest to upload, followed by three to five paid homeowner accounts and one or two serious reviewer or agency conversations. Treat those as validation goals, not promises.

## Weekly measurement

Track:

- Facebook and Instagram reach, saves, comments, and link clicks.
- Landing-page visits by audience.
- Signup starts and completed signups.
- Utility-export uploads.
- Pricing-page visits.
- Paid subscriptions.
- Reviewer and agency inquiries.
- The question or objection that appears most often.

The most important question each week is: **Did the content bring in someone who had a real usage question and help that person take the next step?**
